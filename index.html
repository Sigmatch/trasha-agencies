Why Nakuru County

The highlands are quietly filling up

From Njoro to Gilgil, Lanet and Ngata, our plots sit on cool, fertile ground across Nakuru County — close enough to the city, far enough from the noise.

01

Minutes from Egerton University

A steady stream of students, lecturers and staff keeps rental demand high year-round for anyone building near town.

02

20 minutes to Nakuru CBD

Tarmac along the Nakuru–Njoro–Mau Narok road puts the county capital, markets and hospitals within easy reach.

03

Fertile, well-drained soil

Njoro's red volcanic soil supports wheat, maize, dairy and horticulture — land here works for you even before you build.

04

Cool highland climate

Sitting above 2,100m, Njoro avoids the heat and flooding that trouble lower parts of the county.

05

Piped water & power on-grid

Most of our schemes sit along existing water and electricity lines, cutting your connection costs significantly.

06

Genuine, searchable titles

Every plot is registered at the Nakuru Lands office. We encourage buyers to conduct an independent search before paying.

The Process

From site visit to title deed

  1. 01

    Visit the site

    We pick you up in Njoro or Nakuru town and walk the actual plot with you — beacons, access road and neighbours included.

  2. 02

    Reserve your plot

    Pay a booking fee to hold your chosen plot while your lawyer or our office conducts a search at the Lands office.

  3. 03

    Pay — cash or instalments

    Settle in full or spread payments over up to 6 months. We issue receipts and a sale agreement at every stage.

  4. 04

    Transfer & collect your title

    Once payment is complete, we process the transfer and hand you a registered title deed in your name.
